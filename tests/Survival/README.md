# The Survival Analysis menu: checks by calculation

Build the program, and then run with the .NET 10 SDK:

```
dotnet build StatsDirectUI/StatsDirectUI.csproj -c Debug
dotnet run --project tests/Survival -c Release
```

The checks are made on a build of the program, as those of `tests/CoxRegression` are: `StatsDirect.dll` of the Debug configuration, or of the folder given with `-p:StatsDirectBin=...`. They take about a minute. One part can be run alone by naming it: `km`, `logrank`, `wei` or `life`.

**Kaplan-Meier estimates** (`KaplanMeier.cs`). Sets of records with and without groups, with tied times, with codes above 1 and with blank cells. The estimate of the proportion surviving is compared with the product of the fractions kept as whole numbers, so that a proportion of exactly a half is known to be one; its variance with Greenwood's formula; the median with the first time at which the proportion is a half or less, and the two intervals for it with their definitions; the mean with the area under the curve, and its variance; the rows saved to the worksheet with the limits from log(-log S). Records with a blank time, code or group are left out and counted in a note, and each group keeps its own label.

**Log-rank and generalised Wilcoxon tests** (`LogRank.cs`, `LatentRoots.cs`). Sets of records in two to five groups, with and without strata, tied times, codes above 1 and blank cells, for each of the three kinds of weight. The deaths expected, the rank statistics and their matrix of variances and covariances are worked out from the numbers at risk at each time; the chi-square as the quadratic form in the generalised inverse of the whole matrix, from its latent roots and vectors by Jacobi's method, where the program inverts the matrix without its last row and column; the test for trend; the hazard ratios and their approximate limits. For two groups the hazard ratio by conditional maximum likelihood, its exact limits (Fisher and mid-P) and its P values are compared with the distribution of the deaths of the first group, which is made by putting together the distributions of the tables of all the times. The weight of Peto and Prentice is taken from its definition for times without ties, the deaths of a time being taken one after another; the weight of the time is that of the first of them.

**Wei-Lachin tests** (`WeiLachin.cs`). Sets of subjects with one to four repeats, with tied times, censoring and missing times. The statistic of each repeat is worked out from its definition; the chi-square of each repeat, the omnibus chi-square on the rank of the matrix, and the test of stochastic ordering from the statistics and the matrix that are printed. The variances are checked by drawing sets of subjects without a difference between the groups, and seeing that the statistics have the distributions that they are referred to. The variance of the log-rank statistic is a little small in small samples, and the checks allow for that.

**Life tables** (`LifeTables.cs`, `LifeTableLimits.cs`). For the follow-up life table, the adjusted numbers at risk, the probabilities, the proportions surviving with Greenwood's standard errors and the limits from log(-log) of the proportion. For the abridged life table, every column of the table from its definition, the variances of the probabilities of dying and of the expectations of life from their formulas, the median, and the limits by simulation against a simulation made here, in which the deaths are drawn from the normal distribution with the mean and variance of the Poisson; the limits by simulation should be no narrower than those by formula, which leave out the uncertainty of the death rate of the open interval. Data that a table cannot be made from are refused with a message: blank cells, a population or a length that is not above zero, an open interval without deaths, columns of the wrong lengths, deaths or withdrawals below zero, fewer subjects at the start than leave the table.

The distribution functions that the checks use are worked out in `Program.cs` from series and continued fractions, so that nothing of the program's own arithmetic is relied upon. The run is the same every time, but for the limits by simulation of the abridged life table, which the program draws afresh at each run and which are checked within the tolerance of a simulation. The exit code is 0 if every check passes.
