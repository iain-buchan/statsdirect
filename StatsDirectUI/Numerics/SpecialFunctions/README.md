# Incomplete beta, inverse beta, F and Student t

`IncompleteBeta708.cs`, `MathSupport.cs` and `GammaCoefficients.cs` compute the incomplete beta function ratio, its complement and their logarithms by the methods of DiDonato and Morris, *Significant digit computation of the incomplete beta function ratios*, NSWC TR 88-365 (1988), the report behind ACM Algorithm 708 ([a public copy of the report](https://www.statisticshowto.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/pdf.pdf); [the 1992 paper](https://doi.org/10.1145/131766.131776)). They are written from the report, under StatsDirect's MIT licence, with their own elementary and gamma function helpers.

| Method | Basis |
|---|---|
| Power series | Report equation 12: the integrated binomial series |
| Shape recurrence | Equation 13: a finite sum of positive terms |
| Incomplete gamma expansion | Equation 14, normalised by its first term |
| Weighted continued fraction | Equations 15.1 to 15.3, evaluated by the modified Lentz method |
| Central asymptotic expansion | Equation 16, with coefficients from Lagrange inversion |
| Log-gamma | NIST DLMF 5.7.3 and 5.11.1 |
| Incomplete gamma | NIST DLMF 8.7.3 and 8.9.2 |

The log-gamma Taylor coefficients are zeta values generated by `generate_constants.py`, which writes `GammaCoefficients.cs`.

`BetaInverse.cs` inverts the ratio by Newton's method in log odds within a bracket, solving for the smaller of the two tails and keeping both coordinates with their logarithms, so a quantile whose coordinate underflows still has a finite logarithm; a coordinate below exp(-708) is evaluated from its logarithm by the power series. The inverse reports a failure to bracket or to converge rather than an answer.

`BetaDistributions.cs` gives the F and Student t tails and quantiles, each tail directly and as a logarithm, and keeps the routines the rest of the program calls (`betain`, `xinbta`, `fvalp`, `ffromp`, `tvalp`, `tfromp`, `tfromp2`) with their argument orders and fault codes: `xinbta` accepts the probabilities 0 and 1, and `fvalp` still refuses a negative F. An infinite number of degrees of freedom is taken as 1e20 times the other number, which gives the chi-square and normal limits to double precision; far beyond the range where that stands for infinity (an F below 1e-3 with an infinite numerator, or above 1e3 with an infinite denominator) the tail comes from the incomplete gamma function directly and a quantile that far out is inverted from it. The program's chi-square and gamma tails and quantiles (`chivalp`, `gammad`, `ppchi2`, and the calculator's PCHISQ and QCHISQ) are the F on the same degrees of freedom and an infinite denominator.

The routines were checked against R 4.6.1 and, where R disagreed, against high-precision references, over shapes and degrees of freedom up to 1e12, through the tests in `tests/DistributionRegression`, the program's reports and calculator, and the help's worked examples. The non-central t keeps its own routine.
