##[Unreleased]

### Fixed
- F, Student's t and the incomplete beta ratio, everywhere they are used: the ratio is computed by the methods of DiDonato and Morris (NSWC report TR 88-365, the methods behind ACM Algorithm 708), which keep every figure at large degrees of freedom (the F tail at 100,000 degrees of freedom was right to 11 figures, at 100 million to 8; both are now right to at least 14) and give the smaller tail and its logarithm directly, so a small tail is never lost as one minus the larger and a log probability no longer becomes minus infinity when the probability itself underflows. Its inverse finds the quantile by Newton's method in log odds within a bracket and reports a failure to converge instead of returning its last trial value, so F and t quantiles, and the confidence limits built on them, are right at extreme probabilities and degrees of freedom: the exact 99.8% limits of a rate ratio for one event in 100 person-time units against 50,000,000 in 150 were 3.00E-11 to 1.92E-07 where 3.00E-11 to 2.77E-07 is right, with no error reported, and the F quantile for a lower tail of 0.5% or less with 1 numerator degree of freedom on 5,000 or more denominator degrees of freedom was up to 24% out (1.19E-06 for a lower tail of 0.1% on 1 and 1,000,000 degrees of freedom, where 1.57E-06 is right). The calculator's F and t functions use the same routines, keep the figures of a probability entered as a logarithm, above 2 million degrees of freedom no longer fall back to an approximation (QT) or give up (QF: an upper tail of 0.0005 on 1 and 100,000,000 degrees of freedom had no answer; it is 12.1157), and give the normal and chi-square limits for infinite degrees of freedom, where PT gave 0.5 for every t, PF a tail of 0 or 1, or none, for every F, and QT and QF no answer; TTAIL and INVTTAIL of a missing value are missing (TTAIL gave 1). No report line in the help's worked examples changes; the last of a quantile's 15 figures can move by one, as the t for an upper tail of 0.025 on 20 degrees of freedom, 2.08596344726586 exactly, now shows as 2.08596344726587

##[v5.0.7] 2026-09-25

The F distribution tail at extreme arguments, the normality tests' moments and P values, and the test workbook's population pyramid columns brought up to the mid-2024 estimates in every age band.

### Fixed
- Test workbook: the population pyramid columns of the Graphics worksheet (UK Mid-98 Age Bands, Males, Females, Persons) lacked the 5-9 and 10-14 age bands; they are replaced by the Office for National Statistics mid-2024 estimates for the United Kingdom in all 19 five-year bands, in thousands (UK Mid-2024 Age Bands, Males, Females, Persons), and the help's population pyramid example follows them
- F distribution: an F so large that the product of the numerator degrees of freedom and F overflows gave an upper tail of 0 since 5.0.6 (and an undefined value before); the tail is not 0 when the denominator degrees of freedom are small (7.9e-155 for F = 1e308 on 30 and 1 degrees of freedom, 3.5e-16 on 5 and 0.1). The reciprocal ratio dfd / (dfn F) is now formed directly for a ratio above 1, so every F up to the largest double gives its tail
- Normality tests: the mean and the central moments behind the skewness and kurtosis tests are formed from the values taken relative to the first one and then about the provisional mean, with compensated sums, and the deviations are scaled before they are raised to powers. A sample with a large common offset (readings near 100,000,000 spread over 1) lost figures in the deviations from its mean, and the skewness test's P was off in the sixth figure (0.519622 where 0.519624 is right); values of 1e75 and above overflowed the fourth moment and were refused as too large, a spread of 1e-80 overflowed the kurtosis and a spread of 1e-150 underflowed the cubes, so that a normal sample was reported as unlikely to be normal. The P values of the skewness and kurtosis tests are formed from the upper tail directly: a P below about 1e-16 printed as 0, and one of 1e-8 was off in the ninth figure. Ordinary samples are unchanged
- Normality tests: values alternating between -1e307 and 1e307 overflowed the sum behind the mean, and the report then said that all the values were the same. The values are scaled by their widest difference before the mean is formed, so any sample whose differences the program can hold is tested, and only a difference beyond the largest double is refused as too large
- Normality tests: the Shapiro-Wilk and Shapiro-Francia statistics take the ordered values to the unit interval before the correlation with the scores (W does not depend on location or scale), so values near the largest double no longer overflow them; and a P value that could not be calculated is left out of the conclusion, which said "No non-normality detected" for 100 values alternating between -1e307 and 1e307 where W = 0.636401 and P < 0.0001

- Installing a build over another build with the same version number (a test build, or an unsigned build before the signed one) left the old StatsDirect.exe and StatsDirect.dll on disk, because Windows Installer does not replace a file with one of the same version; the installer now removes a same-version build first, as it does older versions
- After an upgrade, an entry for StatsDirect 4 could stay in Windows' list of installed apps beside the new version, and its Uninstall asked for the original StatsDirectSetup.exe. A version 4 setup can only remove itself with its own copy of Microsoft's .Net installer, which versions 4.0.0 to 4.0.4 kept in one shared folder that the first upgrade between them deleted; this also happened when 4.0.5 replaced such a setup. The installer now retires a version 4 setup that cannot remove itself (its cached files and its registration, never the .Net runtime), and one that would uninstall the .Net 10 runtime; other version 4 setups remove themselves as before
- The installer's own runtime check (used when the .msi is run without the setup executable) accepted a PC that has only a later major version of .Net, on which StatsDirect cannot start; it now applies the program's own roll-forward policy
- The installer's own runtime check still asked for .NET 6, so a .msi run on its own would have installed on a machine whose runtime is too old to start the program; it now requires the .NET 10 Desktop Runtime and says so
