# Changelog

Changelog best practices reference: https://keepachangelog.com/en/1.0.0/

##[v5.0.4] 2026-09-23

### Fixed
- Latin square: the operation listed its report twice, so the analysis appeared twice, and the sums of squares, mean squares and F ratios were printed unrounded
- Latin square: the row, column and treatment codes were shifted by the smallest code among the first n rows only and then used as array indexes, so any row order in which the first n rows did not hold each factor's smallest code, or codes that were not consecutive integers, gave negative sums of squares, "P = *" or a crash. The codes are now ranked to 1..n whatever they are and wherever they fall. A layout that is not a Latin square (a row and column combination missing or repeated, or a treatment not once in every row and once in every column), a 2 by 2 square, or non-numeric codes are refused with a message
- Crossover: the treatment-period interaction block (sum 1 - sum 2, its SE and t) was printed unrounded, and "diff 1 - diff 2" had a stray double space
- Crossover: a missing value in a baseline column was not treated as missing, so that subject's period means became enormous and every test was wrong. Such a subject is now left out, as one with a missing drug or placebo value already was, and the report says how many subjects were left out
- Two way ANOVA with replicates: a missing repeat observation was replaced by the mean of the others in its cell, as the help says, but the residual and total degrees of freedom were not reduced, so the residual mean square was too small and all three F ratios too large. The degrees of freedom are now reduced by the number of substitutions, and the warning says so
- Tukey multiple comparisons: |q*| was printed as an asterisk with four or more groups, because the routine that bracketed its root used (k - 1)/(2k) where the number of pairwise comparisons k(k - 1)/2 was meant. It is now taken as the Studentized range point divided by root 2, which is its value for the equal correlations that routine was given, and the bracketing is corrected as well
- Tukey, Scheffe and Dunnett after a two way ANOVA (the follow-on the two way topic offers): the two way residual mean square was used with the one way residual degrees of freedom, which are larger, so the critical values and P values were too small; after a replicated two way the standard errors also took the number of blocks for the number of observations per treatment. All five methods now take the residual degrees of freedom and the observations per treatment from the analysis they follow
- Tukey and Scheffe crashed (an exception while sorting the contrasts) when the within groups mean square was zero with three or more groups; they now report the contrasts with an infinite statistic and P < 0.0001, as Bonferroni and Dunnett already did and Newman-Keuls now does too. All three refuse a design with no residual degrees of freedom (one observation per group) instead of printing asterisks
- Newman-Keuls: the summary of significant contrasts counted a pair of means as different when its own P value was below the level, even though a wider range of ordered means containing the pair was not significant, contradicting the "stop" marker and the step-down rule. The summary now applies the rule, and since the Newman-Keuls P values are not in order, its single "stop" marker is replaced by "not tested" on every pair inside a wider range that is not significant; the P values are printed as before
- Studentized range: with two means the quantile and probability now come exactly from Student's t, and with three or more means and fewer than five residual degrees of freedom from direct numerical integration. The series routine was accurate only to two or three decimals there (22.56 printed for the 99% point of four means at two residual degrees of freedom, where 22.29 is right), which affected Tukey and Newman-Keuls on very small designs
- Dunnett multiple comparisons: the critical value and the P values were accurate only to about four significant figures (2.452195 where 2.452127 is right, for three treatments and 36 residual degrees of freedom; P = 0.0005 where it is 0.0004), because the integration over the residual standard deviation cut off five millionths of its distribution at each end; the cut-off is now a ten-thousand-millionth. Above 360 residual degrees of freedom the P values also switched to a known-variance approximation while the critical value did not until 3600, so a contrast could be called significant while its own interval included zero; both now use the exact integral up to ten million degrees of freedom. The values agree with direct numerical integration to seven figures throughout, the integration being finer below five degrees of freedom. The method is now available from two residual degrees of freedom (it needed four), and refusing it says why
- Squared ranks equality of variance test with three or more samples: the pairwise contrasts were printed whenever the overall P was below 0.95, that is almost always, instead of when the overall test rejected at the chosen level
- Bartlett's test formed the group variances from raw sums of squares and lost figures when the values were large compared with their spread (0.505718 for 0.343929 with values near 100,000,000); the variances are now formed about the group means, as Levene's and Welch's already were
- One way analysis of variance: the within groups sum of squares is accumulated from the deviations about the group means rather than taken as the total less the between groups sum, which loses figures when the groups account for nearly all of the variation; the pooled variance used by the multiple comparison methods is found the same way
- Bonferroni: the "critical P for k comparisons" line always divided 0.05 by k whatever confidence level was chosen; it now divides 1 minus the chosen level
- Nested ANOVA: a subgroup with no observations gave a report with empty cells; it is now refused with a message
- Messages: the equality of variance tests said "zero length group" for a group with one observation, and Dunnett said "Fault in calculation" for too few degrees of freedom

### Changed
- The Tukey and Newman-Keuls tables head their statistic column "q", the Studentized range statistic (the difference divided by the standard error of a mean), rather than "|L/SE(L)|", which is the Scheffe table's statistic and root 2 smaller
- The grouped one way and two way ANOVA reports (not on the menu) print rounded figures
- The Tukey, Scheffe and Dunnett tables put a space between a difference and its confidence interval
##[v5.0.3] 2026-09-22

### Fixed
- The check for a new version at start-up still did not run in 5.0.1 and 5.0.2. The registry helper that reads the CheckForUpdates value returned 0, not "absent", when the value does not exist: in "val is null ? default : (int)val" the default literal takes the type of the other branch, int. Version 5.0.1 corrected the test on the result but not the helper, and that correction was checked by reading the code rather than by running it. This time the path was traced in a running copy: the check runs, and its dialog is shown once the opening dialog has closed. Installed copies of 5.0.1 and 5.0.2 will therefore not announce 5.0.3 by themselves: use Help > Check for Updates
- The application object was created without thread safety, and the background check reaches it from a thread-pool thread about a second after start, before the main thread does; each could have created its own copy, and a dialog waiting in the copy that lost would never have been shown
- Exceptions swallowed by the main window's start-up handler, and the steps of the start-up check, are now written to StatsDirect-diagnostics.log in the temporary folder, so that "nothing happened" can be traced

##[v5.0.2] 2026-09-22

Version 5.0.2 extends the check described under 5.0.1 to the Parametric and Descriptive menus and to the Gini coefficient: each function's worked example and a range of other data were run through the program's own report and through R, and two independent reviews read every changed routine and ran about 300 further cases. The faults found are corrected below; two of them (the BCa interval of the Gini coefficient, and the unpaired t test when the mean is large compared with the spread) change results for data a user could well have. The web help now ends every worked example of the Parametric and Descriptive sections with R code that reproduces it, including the plots the report draws, and three topics that had no worked example (F test, z tests, summary data t tests) have one.

### Changed
- Shapiro-Wilk and Shapiro-Francia tests: the normal score for each ordered value is now taken from its position, as in the published algorithms (Royston 1995 and 1983) and in R; tied values had been given the same score, from their mid-rank. W, V and P change only when there are ties: for the help's example (two values of -0.0363) W is 0.892184 with P = 0.0054 where 0.892516 and 0.0055 were printed, and W' is 0.873427 with P = 0.0034 where 0.873776 and 0.0035 were printed. Without ties nothing changes

### Fixed
- Unpaired t test, from data or from summary statistics: t was always printed as a positive number, while the confidence interval for the difference between means carried the sign of the difference (first sample less second), so the two disagreed whenever the first sample had the smaller mean (t = 1.891436 with an interval of -40.193679 to 2.193679 for the help's example with the columns selected in the other order). Both t and t(d) now carry the same sign as the interval (-1.891436). The P values, the power and the F test are unchanged
- Paired t test with no variation in the differences: identical columns gave "t = *" and "One sided P < 0.0001"; P is now not calculated either. Differences that are all the same non-zero value give an infinite t with P < 0.0001, as the single sample test reports them
- Normality tests on a column of identical values (or a single value) stopped with an exception from the normal plot ("Cannot create a suitable axis"); the report now says that normality cannot be tested because there is no variation. The skewness and kurtosis lines now have a comma before their P value, as the omnibus test line has and as the help shows
- Normal plot with Blom scores (the normality report's plot, and the Blom option of the normal plot on the Graphics menu): the score for the value of rank r was taken as the normal deviate of r / (n + 1), which is van der Waerden's score, so the Blom and van der Waerden options drew the same plot. Blom's score, the normal deviate of (r - 3/8) / (n + 1/4), is now used, which moves the points a little towards the ends of the line
- Gini coefficient, BCa confidence interval: the acceleration constant comes from the coefficients with each value left out in turn. Those were calculated with the ranks and n of the full sample, so they were wrong, and the cubed differences in the formula had the opposite sign to Efron and Tibshirani's (and to the help's technical note). The BCa limits were therefore off for all data: for one sample of 20 values 0.443 to 0.671 was printed (100,000 re-samples) where 0.484 to 0.711 is right. The coefficient, its unbiased form and the percentile interval were right
- Gini coefficient: zero values were included in the calculation although the help says, and the report's count is labelled, that only positive values are used (and re-samples made up of zeros gave "Bias = *"). They are now left out and counted with the observations not used
- Single sample z test: the sample mean was printed unrounded and followed by ":roundu" (a mistyped placeholder in the report template), and the interval printed beside the geometric mean, exp(mean of logs plus or minus z times their sd), was labelled a confidence interval; it is the reference range the help describes
- Unpaired t test from data with a mean that is large compared with the spread (values of about 1e5 times their standard deviation or more, such as timestamps or large readings with a small spread): the pooled sums of squares were formed from the raw sums, which loses digits, so t, both standard errors, both P values and both intervals drifted (t = -1.07736 where -1.077632 is right for five values near 1e7; wrong from about 1e8; t = -infinity and P < 0.0001 at 1e9). They are now formed about the means, as the standard deviations already were. The single sample and paired tests, and the tests from summary statistics, were not affected
- Shapiro-Wilk and Shapiro-Francia tests with a mean that is large compared with the spread: the correlation behind W and W' was formed from raw sums in the same way (W = 0.937173 where 0.936739 is right for 20 values near 1e6; 0.926823 where 0.921361 near 1e7, which changed the report's conclusion). It is now formed about the means. The same routine set a correlation of -1 to +1; only the sign given to the principal components used it
- Normality tests: an adjusted P below about 1e-16 left the omnibus chi-square unadjusted (278.17 printed for 15 zeros and 15 ones, where the adjusted value is 168.41; the P was < 0.0001 either way), and so did a raw chi-square above about 1490, whose P underflows a step earlier (1,780 printed for 17 zeros and 17 ones where the adjusted value is 1,047); the adjustment is now made in log space whatever the size; a kurtosis so far below normal that the approximation's cube root has a negative base (two point data with more than about 35 values) gave "P = *" for the kurtosis and the omnibus test, and now gives P < 0.0001; with 3 observations the Shapiro-Wilk P used a polynomial approximation to the arcsine, out by up to 0.00005 and occasionally negative (P = 0.0847 printed where 0.0848 is right); values of size above about 1e75 are now reported as too large rather than "all the same"; 2 observations get "too few observations" rather than "Error in calculation"
- Paired t test with the agreement analysis option and differences that do not vary (identical columns, a constant shift, or a single pair): the report stopped with an exception from the agreement plot. The limits of agreement are now printed with a note that there is no plot
- Unpaired t test: when the F test cannot be calculated (a sample with one value, or two constant samples) the report said that the two sided F test is not significant and that there is no need to assume unequal variances; it now says that the F test was not calculated
- Poisson confidence interval: a confidence level of 50% or less blanked the mean (the one sided interval, which does not exist at such a level, is still blank); and a non-integer total between 0.9 and 1.1 was given the lower limit for a total of exactly 1
- Reference range: the minimum of 8 observations was checked on the column length including blank cells
- Time series summary with a missing observation: the subject's minimum was printed as "*" (the missing value code was taken as the minimum), and the area under the curve left out the whole slice belonging to the missing time (each time's slice being the observation times half the gap between its neighbours), so it was too small: 253.95 for a subject whose 45 minute value was missing where the trapezium rule over the observations present gives 289.28. The area is now the trapezium rule over the observations the subject has, which is unchanged when none is missing
- A chart that cannot be drawn no longer loses the report: values near 1e8 or more with a small spread broke the axis layout of the normal plot with an exception, and the whole normality report with it. The report now prints "(Chart not drawn: ...)" in place of any chart that fails, with the tests intact

##[v5.0.1] 2026-09-22

Version 5.0.1 follows a check of every function on the Nonparametric menu, one at a time, against R and against exact calculations: the help's worked example and a range of other data (ties, missing values, very small and very large samples, values of unusual size, degenerate data) were run through the program's own report and through R, and an independent review then read every changed routine and ran about 190 further cases. The faults found are corrected below; two of them (the slope interval of nonparametric linear regression, at the top of the interval and with more than about 130 pairs) change results for ordinary data. The web help now ends each worked example of the Nonparametric section with R code that reproduces it, and those scripts, the help's examples and the program's reports are checked against one another automatically.

### Changed
- Mann-Whitney and Wilcoxon signed ranks reports: K is printed again beside the confidence interval for the difference (K = 134 and K = 14 in the help's examples), as the help describes and as earlier versions did; the interval runs from the Kth smallest to the Kth largest difference (or average of two differences). An approximate K is labelled "(approx)": in the signed ranks test that is from 200 pairs, where K comes from a normal approximation, and above 1000 pairs the confidence level is headed "Approximate", because it is then the level asked for rather than the level achieved

### Fixed
- The check for a new version at start-up never ran. It runs unless a registry value (CheckForUpdates) is present and zero, but nothing writes that value, and since version 4.0.5 an absent value has been read as "do not check", so no installed copy ever looked. Had it looked, the result would have come back on a background thread before the main window existed, and the dialog would have been put on the wrong thread. The check now runs when the value is absent, and a newer version is announced once the main window is on the screen, after the opening dialog. Help > Check for Updates was not affected. Because of this, version 5.0.0 does not announce 5.0.1 by itself: use Help > Check for Updates
- Nonparametric linear regression with many pairs: the quantile of Kendall's statistic that sets the confidence limits of the slope was searched for only up to a score of 1000, which it passes at about 133 pairs for a 95% interval (111 at 99%). Beyond that the interval was too narrow, and more so the larger the sample: for one set of 200 pairs a "95%" interval of 0.240 to 0.320 was printed where 0.201 to 0.353 is right, and for 400 pairs 0.298 to 0.316 where 0.257 to 0.355 is right. Such intervals cover the true slope about 71% of the time at 200 pairs and about 29% at 400. The search now runs to the largest possible score. The same quantile gives the critical values of Kendall's tau in the Distributions dialog, which were wrong for the same sample sizes. The slope, the intercept and the rank correlation were right. Present in version 5.0.0 and earlier
- Data > Pairwise slopes with a median slope and confidence interval: the tool had its own copy of the slope interval and now gives the same limits as the regression report (0 to 0.008 for the help's example, where it gave 0 to 0.0075), says when there are too few pairs for an interval, and does not write a slope of minus zero
- Kendall's rank correlation: with fewer than two rows that have a value in both columns the report still gave "z (continuity corrected) = -infinity" and "Two sided P < 0.0001"; it now says that there are too few pairs. With a score of exactly zero the continuity corrected z was -1 divided by the standard error where it should be zero (two sided P 0.73 where it is 1 for four pairs); the same correction is in the nonparametric linear regression report
- Wilcoxon signed ranks test: the normal approximation was chosen on the number of pairs, including pairs that do not differ, not on the number of differences that are ranked, so 196 identical pairs and 5 differences got a normal P from five ranks (two sided 0.0431 where the exact P is 0.0625). It is now chosen when more than 200 differences are ranked
- Spearman's rank correlation with ties and rho of exactly 1 or -1: all three P values were printed as "P = *"; the t approximation gives 0 and 1 there
- Mann-Whitney and Wilcoxon signed ranks confidence intervals with values of unusual size: the data are scaled to whole numbers, and the scale was chosen from the largest value, not the largest in size, so negative values below about -21,000 lost the whole Mann-Whitney report, P values included; values above about 200,000,000 gave no interval; and values of about 0.00001 or smaller gave limits of 0. The scale is now chosen from the largest value in size and can rise for very small values; results for ordinary data are unchanged
- Chi-square goodness of fit: expected totals of 2,147,483,648 or more stopped the analysis; expected values adding up to zero gave a report of asterisks; negative observed counts were accepted. Each now gives a message, and large totals are handled. The simulated exact P follow-on, and the column of category names, are also hardened in the same way as the main calculation (four entries below) for a blank cell or a short column, which the worksheet does not let through
- Quantile confidence interval with more than 200 observations: the conservative option was ignored although the line was labelled "(conservative)"; each limit is now moved outwards until no more than half of the remaining probability lies beyond it, as for smaller samples (95.98% for the median of 400 values where the unconservative interval has 94.90%)
- Nonparametric linear regression with every outcome value the same now gives a message (the rank correlation was 0/0)
- Nonparametric linear regression, confidence interval for the slope: the upper limit was taken one ordered slope too low, so the interval was too short at the top and its coverage fell below the level asked for (94.6% for a 95% interval with 12 pairs, where Conover's rule gives 95.5%). With N slopes and w the quantile of Kendall's statistic the limits are the rth smallest and the rth largest slope, r = (N - w) / 2 rounded down; the ((N + w) / 2)th smallest had been used for the upper limit in place of the (N + 1 - r)th. For the help's example the interval is 0 to 0.008, as Conover (1999) and the help's own discussion give it, where 0 to 0.0075 was printed. The lower limit, the slope, the intercept and the rank correlation are unchanged. A predictor column with every value the same stopped the analysis with an index out of range and now gives a message; a slope of minus zero was printed as "-0". With too few pairs for an interval at the level asked for (5 pairs at 99%, or fewer slopes because of ties in the predictor) the report printed false limits such as "0 to 0"; it now shows no limits and says that there are too few pairs
- Chi-square goodness of fit: expected values entered as percentages adding up to 100, as the help's example states them, no longer bring the warning that the expected total differs from the observed total. The calculation is also hardened for two cases that the worksheet does not let through, a blank cell and columns of different lengths: a row with a blank cell was left out of the comparison but still counted in the totals, so the chi-square was wrong (20.45 where 17.05 is right for the help's example with such a row added), and unequal columns stopped the calculation with an index out of range. Neither could be reached from the worksheet, which refuses blank cells for this analysis and requires the columns to be the same length; they were found by the test harness, which drives the calculation directly
- Friedman, Cuzick and nonparametric linear regression reports: uneven spacing ("T2 (F) =  3.192198", "Ez =  3.022222", "z   = 2.114639", "tau b =  0.439039"), and the tie-corrected variance in the Cuzick report is labelled Var(T) like the uncorrected one (it was "VarT")
- Quantile confidence interval with more than 200 observations: the "exact confidence level" was worked out from a different term of the binomial distribution from the one the limits use, so it could be out by one term (95.48% reported for an interval from the 349th to the 372nd of 400 ordered values, whose level is 94.54%). The limits themselves, and everything for 200 observations or fewer, are unchanged
- Smirnov two sample test: a one sided P above 0.5 was shown as "P > 0.4999", a leftover from when the one sided P was half of the two sided P; the exact value is now shown (0.9644 for a sample shifted the other way)
- Kruskal-Wallis test and its pairwise comparisons with every observation the same: the report gave mean ranks of 0 and T = -3(N + 1), a negative chi-square; it now says that the test cannot be calculated. The names in the titles of the pairwise comparisons are spelt Critchlow and Iman (they were "Chritchlow", and "Inman" in the one sided report)
- Spearman's rank correlation: with ties the two one sided P values were printed as "P = *"; they now come from the same t approximation as the two sided P (0.0109 and 0.9891 for a tied sample of 8 where the two sided P is 0.0219). With 3 pairs the report gave a confidence interval of "-1 to *", where Fisher's interval does not exist; with rho equal to 1 or -1 it could give "1 to 1" instead of saying that the interval is not calculated, because rho worked out from ranks can miss 1 by a rounding error. Results without ties and with 4 or more pairs are unchanged
- Spearman and Kendall rank correlation with a column in which every value is the same: Kendall's report gave a continuity corrected z of minus infinity with "Two sided P < 0.0001", a significant result from data that cannot be ranked, and Spearman's analysis stopped with an arithmetic overflow. Both now say that rank correlation cannot be calculated when all of the values in a column are the same
- Spearman and Kendall rank correlation reports: uneven spacing around the equals signs ("(Rho) =  0.684848", "Concordant pairs  =  34", "Kendall's tau  = 0.511111"), and "Can not" for "Cannot" in two reports. No figure changes
- Wilcoxon signed ranks test with more than 200 pairs, where a normal approximation is used: the one sided P values were wrong, because the statistic was divided by the sum of the squared ranks rather than its square root, and the upper side took a lower tail area when the statistic was negative; both came out close to 0.5 (for 201 pairs with a normalised statistic of 1.980751 the lower side P was 0.5005 where 0.9762 is right; with the columns exchanged both sides were 0.4995 where 0.0238 and 0.9762 are right). The two sided P was right, and so was the upper side P when the statistic was positive. The sum of ranks for positive differences was printed under the label "Sum of signed ranks for all differences", and the line for the normalised statistic had a misplaced space when there were ties. The exact test, used for up to 200 pairs, is unchanged
- Wilcoxon signed ranks test and Mann-Whitney test, confidence interval with very small samples: when even the widest possible interval falls short of the confidence asked for (4 or 5 pairs at 95%, 7 or fewer at 99%; 4 and 4 observations at 99%), the report gave a "100% confidence interval" with an upper limit a little above the largest difference (-2 to 5.000005 for the pairs 1, 3, 0, 5 and 0, 0, 2, 0, where -2 to 5 has 87.5% confidence). It now says that the interval is not calculated and why. The message for fewer than 4 observations in the Mann-Whitney report had lost its ending ("not calculated if n1 or n2")

##[v5.0.0] 2026-09-20

Version 5 follows an independent audit of the calculation layer against R (and more accurate references where R has none), which found 24 defects. A second audit on 19 September 2026, again against R, and the reviews of its fixes found more; they are listed below with the rest. All are corrected here, and each correction was confirmed on the Windows build. Results from the procedures listed below can differ from version 4.

### Changed
- Intraclass correlation (Agreement > Continuous) is the one-way ANOVA estimator ICC(1) with an exact F-based confidence interval, as R and Shrout and Fleiss, not Fisher's symmetrical-table form
- Power for the t tests uses the exact non-central t distribution; sample sizes for the paired t test, independent proportions and correlation are one smaller in some cases
- Two-sample Smirnov P values are exact and conditional on ties, as R's ks.test, at any sample size
- Harbord's test uses the hypergeometric variance of the score for odds-ratio analyses; the exact confidence interval for Hedges g converges to 1e-9
- Meta-analysis bias indicators: the confidence intervals for the Egger and Harbord-Egger bias estimates are given at twice the alpha of the analysis, so 90% by default, matching the P < 0.1 convention for these low-powered tests. Egger's interval was previously at the level of the analysis (95%); Harbord's was at 92.5% by mistake, where earlier versions and the help gave 90%. The bias estimates and P values are unchanged
- A Bonferroni-adjusted (simultaneous) confidence interval is added to the Bonferroni multiple comparison report
- Time series summary: the precision of the mean AUC comes from the subjects' own AUCs, the group comparison is a Welch t test, and the bootstrap resamples whole subjects
- The multi-rater kappa report no longer prints the line labelled weighted kappa, which was a Berry-Mielke coefficient on per-column codes
- Bootstrap and random allocation draws are uniform, so bootstrap and randomisation results differ from version 4 even with the same seed
- DevExpress.Win.RichEdit updated to 26.1.5
- SpreadsheetGear updated to 9.3.84
- Dependencies updated
- The setup executable bundles the .Net 10.0.12 Windows Desktop runtime, up from 10.0.0
- LOESS: the default polynomial degree is 2, as the help says and as in R itself, not 1
- The installer puts about 490 files on the machine instead of 1,503 and is 6 MB smaller: it no longer carries a superseded copy of the web help, the compiler's messages in twelve other languages, the test framework, a separate debug symbols file or the help maintainer's mapping workbook. Error messages still give source line numbers, because the symbols are now inside StatsDirect.dll
- Signed builds use the StatsDirect Ltd certificate on its USB token, timestamp at GlobalSign, verify each signature, and sign StatsDirect.exe and StatsDirect.dll as well as the installer and setup executable
- The licence shown by setup and in Help is now the MIT License under which StatsDirect is released, followed by the terms of the components from other suppliers (SpreadsheetGear and DevExpress remain proprietary and may not be redistributed), in place of the former proprietary licence. The former licence's exclusion of liability for reliance on the software or its results is kept, in the name of the authors and copyright holders rather than of StatsDirect Limited, which is closing; copyright statements now name Professor Iain E. Buchan at The University of Liverpool, as LICENSE does. LICENSE.txt and THIRD-PARTY-NOTICES.txt are installed with the program, and Help > About shows them
- StatsDirect starts faster and the first analysis of a session is quicker (about a quarter to nearly a half less time in measurements of start-up work); a few calculation-heavy analyses take slightly longer the first time they run. .Net's tiered compilation, switched off since June 2024, is back at its default. Results are unchanged
- The installer no longer tries to generate native images with the .NET Framework's ngen, a step left from version 3 that could do nothing for a .NET 10 program; the About box no longer reports "JIT-compiled"
- Help: the Search tab finds words. The help file now carries a full-text search index, which the version 4 help never had
- Help: a citation in a topic opens the entry it cites in the reference list, not the top of the list. The reference list is in one consistent style, with a DOI link wherever the work has one (257 entries, where 71 had one), and misspelt authors and wrong years in citations are corrected
- Calculator and worksheet functions (User defined function, Fill series, Search and replace): a minus sign now works in front of anything, not only a number: -X1, -PI, -(3+2), -LOG(2), X1^-X2, --5. It binds less tightly than ^ and !, as the help has always said and as in R and Visual Basic, so -2^2 is -4 and -3! is -6, where version 4 gave 4 and an error; 4^-2 is still 0.0625, and a chain of powers is still worked from left to right. Formulas typed into worksheet cells follow Excel instead, where =-2^2 is 4. Also: the factorial of a variable can be written X1!; whole numbers above 2,147,483,647 are accepted; the typographic minus sign that word processors and web pages use is read as a minus sign
- Calculator and worksheet functions: INVPOISSONTAIL(mean, k) is the probability of k or a more extreme number of events, in the direction away from the mean: k or more when k is at or above the mean, k or fewer when it is below. It used to take a probability and return a count, from a search that could never give 0 or 1
- Sample size for survival: the hazard ratio is that of experimental subjects relative to controls, as the prompt says: with exponential survival, the control median divided by the experimental median. Earlier versions read an entered hazard ratio the other way up, so 0.5 gave 49 per group where 75 is right (control median 10, accrual 5, follow-up 10, 80% power, 5% alpha); entering two medians gave the right size but echoed the reciprocal. The report shows both medians and says which way the ratio runs, the number of controls is rounded up, and a design with no accrual and no follow-up is refused instead of printing NaN. The size is rounded as the other sample size functions round it; one subject was added before rounding up, so it is now one fewer than before
- Risk (retrospective): when no population exposure is entered it is estimated from the controls, not from cases and controls pooled, which moved with the number of controls sampled; the attributable risk then belongs with its (Walter) confidence interval, and the report says which exposure was used (51.29% where version 4 gave 55.29% for the help's example). When an exposure is entered, the interval comes from the odds ratio's logit limits; Walter's variance was used there too and could be several times too wide, or too narrow
- Multiple regression: adjusted R² is reported as calculated when it is negative, as R, Stata and SAS do, where earlier versions showed 0%; without an intercept its total degrees of freedom are n, not n - 1, as in R

### Fixed
- Chi-square quantile: at several million degrees of freedom the routine could go round for ever without settling, which froze the program (the 99.9% point with ten million degrees of freedom, from the calculator or the Distributions dialog, and in principle from an interval for a very large count); it now hands over to a bisection after 100 steps, and every calculation that used to finish gives exactly the same answer
- Calculator and Distributions dialog: a mistyped function name is reported as such (it said "Object reference not set to an instance of an object"); a quotation mark or backslash inside a text value is handled (the text after the quotation mark used to be read as part of the formula); PF and FTAIL of a missing value are missing (PF of the missing answer of another function came out as 1), and an F quantile beyond 1e304 is found. In the Distributions dialog a P value typed into the upper tail or two sided box was not noticed unless Invert was pressed, so the report could carry the previous calculation
- Calculator and worksheet functions: a number in an expression was searched for thousands separators, which nobody types in a formula but which an argument list typed without spaces contains. PT(2,120), PBINOM(3,100,0.5) and PNORM(90,100,15) stopped with "input string was not in a correct format", and PT(10,120.25,0.5) was silently worked out as PT(10120.25, 0.5). Numbers are now digits, a decimal separator and an exponent only, as in a spreadsheet formula; an expression that does contain a thousands separator (1,234.5 - 0.5) is refused, with a hint about thousands separators. Where the comma is the decimal separator, PT(2, 10) could not be read at all because "2," was taken for a number; it now can, and a semicolon is accepted between arguments everywhere, so PT(2,5; 10) is unambiguous. For regional settings that group digits with a space, every space used to be removed from an expression before it was read. The built-in unit conversions are written with a decimal point and 60 of the 68 could not be read where the comma is the decimal separator (on the screen version the error closed the program); they are now read the same way everywhere. A negative whole number (-2) could not be evaluated under Norwegian, Swedish or Finnish settings, which write the minus sign typographically
- Chi-square P values: the upper tail area was taken as 1 minus the lower, so no P value could be smaller than about 1e-16 and a large chi-square gave exactly 0 (a chi-square of 80 with 1 degree of freedom, where P is 3.7e-19). It is now computed directly, throughout the program; other P values change, if at all, in the sixteenth decimal place. In the Distributions dialog a second very small P value was not written over the first (a chi-square of 100 after one of 80 went on showing 3.7e-19)
- Calculator functions: the lower tails of PCHISQ and PF were taken as 1 minus the upper and a small one was lost altogether (PCHISQ(5, 100) was 0 where it is 2.2e-46); each tail is now computed as itself, the logarithm of a tail is given even where the tail is too small to hold, and a negative value answers 0 and 1 for the two tails. QCHISQ, INVCHI2TAIL, QF and INVFTAIL gave no answer for a small tail area (INVCHI2TAIL(1, 1e-20) is 87.16) or, for some degrees of freedom, a wrong one (QF with a lower tail area of 1.1e-6 and 1 and 1000 degrees of freedom was negative): a small tail area is now inverted directly, and QF and INVFTAIL keep the usual result only when the tail area at it comes back as asked
- Regression without an intercept: predicting from a logistic model fitted without an intercept stopped with an error, as did prediction and parameter detail (with collinearity) for multiple linear regression; the multiple linear regression report labelled the first slope "Intercept" and left its name out of the equation, and best subset named the wrong predictors and did not offer the first one to be kept. Models with an intercept are unchanged. In multiple linear regression prediction a blank or non-numeric value for a predictor gave a prediction such as -9E+307 with infinite limits, where the prediction and its intervals are now shown as missing
- Calculator functions: ASEC, ACSC and ACOT (and their other names) used formulas that are not the inverses of SEC, CSC and COT, so ASEC(2) was 0.857 where it is 1.047, ACSC(2) 0.857 where it is 0.524 and ACOT(2) 2.678 where it is 0.464; ACOT now runs from 0 to pi and passes through pi/2 at 0. The hyperbolic functions failed at sizes where the answer is an ordinary number: ASINH(-1e8) and ASINH(1e200) were infinite, ASINH(1e-20) and ATANH(1e-20) were 0, TANH(1000) and COTH(1000) were not a number and SINH(710) was infinite. PT and QT with a non-centrality parameter rounded fractional degrees of freedom down without saying so, even for a non-centrality of 0; fractional degrees of freedom are now used as given (QT then gives no answer for a probability within 1e-10 of 0 or 1, which cannot be located reliably). PNORM, UZ, QNORM and IZ lost an upper tail below about 1e-16 (PNORM(10, 0, 1, FALSE) was 0 where it is 7.6e-24; QNORM(1e-20, 0, 1, FALSE) gave no answer), and the logarithm of a probability too small to hold was minus infinity in PNORM, DPOIS and DBINOM and unusable in QNORM
- Calculator and worksheet functions: a character the evaluator did not know, such as a typographic minus sign, an en dash or a multiplication sign pasted from a document, was dropped without a word, so EXP(−X1) was evaluated as EXP(X1). The typographic minus is now understood and any other unknown character is a syntax error
- Calculator and worksheet functions: comparing a whole number with an integer division, as in 3 = 7\2, stopped with an error; checking whether a piece of text is a valid expression could itself stop with an error (for a text search such as abc) instead of answering no
- Search and replace (advanced) evaluated missing cells, so a rule such as X < 0 matched blanks and a replacement such as ABS(X) turned them into 1.8E308; Fill series discarded any formula shorter than three characters, such as -X
- Kruskal-Wallis, Conover-Iman comparisons: with tied data the variance of the ranks and the Kruskal-Wallis statistic in the comparison now allow for the ties, as Conover (1999) and the R packages PMCMRplus and conover.test do; the untied forms were used before, which was slightly conservative (0.0078 where 0.0071 is right for the first comparison in the help's example). Nothing changes without ties
- Random numbers: gamma deviates were generated for a scaling parameter B of zero (a column of zeros) or below zero (negative "gamma" deviates) without complaint; the prompt for the second parameter of beta deviates called it a non-centrality parameter, where it is the second shape parameter

- The Distributions menu said Binomal
- Mann-Whitney test: the exact one sided P values were the wrong way round whenever U was above half of n1 x n2, that is when the first sample tended to be the larger: the smaller tail was always printed as the lower side. The same tolerance applies to unweighted data, where it changes one thing: a user-defined centile for which centile x n is a whole number that the computer's arithmetic just misses (the 29th, 57th or 7th centile of 100 values, where 0.29 x 100 is held as 28.999999999999996) is now the average of the two neighbouring values, as the definition says and as the 10th centile of 100 values always was; the median and quartiles are not affected
